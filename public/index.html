ChassEZE Group · Strategy and execution

ChassEZE Group Business Strategy

Planning horizon: 12 months from [approved start date]

Strategic focus
Build a profitable core, deliver reliably, and expand when customer demand and cash support it.

1. Executive Direction

ChassEZE Group should build its next year around a focused set of commercially viable offers, reliable delivery, and disciplined cash management. The recommended sequence is to establish a clear operating baseline, prove demand and delivery economics, and then expand the offers and channels that produce repeatable results.

This strategy sets out the company overview, strategic goals, operating model, growth priorities, and execution responsibilities. Its central decision is to concentrate resources on one primary customer segment and no more than two priority offers during the first quarter. Leadership should approve further expansion only when customer demand, delivery capacity, and cash forecasts support it.

4. Operating Model and Operations

Group Structure and Accountability

The proposed structure combines shared leadership, finance, and operating standards with clear accountability for each business line. If ChassEZE Group currently operates as a single business, one person may hold multiple roles; record a named owner for every responsibility.

Role Core responsibility Decision authority
[CEO or Managing Director] Strategy, portfolio choices, major partnerships, and resource allocation Approves annual plan, material commitments, and expansion
[Business Unit or Offer Lead] Customer outcomes, offer economics, and delivery performance Manages approved offer scope and budget
[Commercial Lead] Positioning, prospecting, proposals, pricing execution, and pipeline Approves standard commercial terms within delegated limits
[Operations Lead] Capacity, scheduling, quality, suppliers, and process improvement Assigns delivery resources and resolves delivery issues
[Finance Lead] Budgets, collections, cash forecasts, reporting, and financial controls Enforces spending limits and escalates forecast shortfalls

Leadership should publish spending and discount limits in Month 1. Purchases outside budget, contracts above [approval threshold], and exceptions below [minimum gross margin] require approval from [authorized role].

Customer and Delivery Workflow

  1. Qualify demand: Record the customer's need, budget, decision maker, and timing in a shared customer relationship management system or tracker.
  2. Scope and price: Confirm deliverables, exclusions, estimated effort, direct costs, payment terms, and acceptance criteria.
  3. Approve the commitment: Check capacity and margin before issuing the final proposal or accepting the order.
  4. Onboard the customer: Assign an owner, agree milestones, and collect any contracted deposit.
  5. Deliver and review: Track progress weekly, check quality, and document changes to scope, dates, or fees.
  6. Close and collect: Confirm acceptance, invoice promptly, and resolve overdue balances.
  7. Retain and learn: Capture feedback, identify the next relevant need, and request a referral when appropriate.

Maintain concise procedures for these activities, with a checklist, owner, and escalation point for each. Use one shared record for customer commitments and one action register for execution.

5. Growth Priorities

Priority 1 Strengthen the Core Offer

  • Interview at least 10 current or prospective customers by Month 2.
  • Define up to two offers with clear outcomes, scope, pricing, and delivery standards.
  • Build proof from customer-approved results and relevant examples.
  • Review direct costs and margins before changing pricing or adding features.
Success gate
Customers understand the offer, demand is evidenced, and delivery can meet the approved minimum margin.

Priority 2 Build Repeatable Customer Acquisition

  • Test no more than two channels initially, selected for the chosen segment: referrals, direct outreach, partnerships, or targeted content.
  • Track qualified opportunities, proposals, wins, channel costs, and time to close.
  • Document the sales process and follow-up schedule.
  • Increase spending only after a channel produces acceptable economics across multiple completed sales.
Success gate
At least one channel delivers qualified customers consistently over three consecutive months within [approved acquisition cost or payback limit].

Priority 3 Increase Repeat Business

  • Schedule customer reviews at relevant delivery milestones.
  • Introduce renewal reminders or repurchase follow-up aligned with the buying cycle.
  • Offer complementary services only when they solve a validated need.
  • Close feedback issues with a named owner and response date.
Success gate
Retention meets the approved target, and repeat business maintains the minimum margin.

Priority 4 Expand Selectively

  • Assess adjacent customer segments, geographies, or partnerships after the core offer performs reliably.
  • Use a limited pilot with a defined budget, duration, and success criteria.
  • Prefer partners where they improve access or capacity without creating unsustainable commitments.
  • Approve permanent hiring or broader expansion only after reviewing demand, utilization, and cash.
Success gate
A pilot produces paid demand, acceptable delivery quality, and positive contribution after incremental costs, within the approved budget.

Every monthly review should show baseline, target, actual result, trend, accountable owner, and corrective action. Finance should define consistent cost classifications and reconcile financial measures to the accounting records.

7. Practical Execution Plan

🟢 First 30 Days

  • Week 1: Appoint the strategy owner, confirm business lines and customer segments, and gather available financial and operating records.
  • Week 2: Establish KPI baselines, list active commitments, and prepare the first 13-week cash forecast.
  • Week 3: Review customer evidence, rank offers, and estimate the resources needed for the priority initiatives.
  • Week 4: Approve targets, named owners, budget limits, and the first-quarter action register.

🔵 12 Month Action Roadmap

🟠 Milestone Gates

  • End of Month 3: Proceed with broader sales activity only after offer scope, cost estimates, and delivery responsibilities are clear.
  • End of Month 6: Increase acquisition spending only when the channel gate in Section 5 is met and the cash forecast supports it.
  • End of Month 9: Expand a pilot only if paid demand, contribution, delivery quality, and capacity satisfy its pre-agreed criteria.
  • End of Month 12: Allocate next-year resources according to demonstrated offer performance and strategic fit.

If a gate is missed, record the cause, a corrective action, and a new decision date. Avoid treating calendar progress as evidence that an initiative is ready to scale.
